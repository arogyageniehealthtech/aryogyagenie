# Community-Acquired Pneumonia Clinical Guidelines

## Disease Definition & Pathophysiology
Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) is an acute infection of the lung parenchyma caused by bacterial pathogens (such as Streptococcus pneumoniae, Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae) or viral respiratory pathogens. The infection leads to pulmonary alveolar inflammation and fluid consolidation.

## Clinical Symptoms & Warning Signs
Key symptoms and warning signs of acute pneumonia include:
- Productive cough with yellow, green, or rust-colored phlegm (sputum).
- High fever, severe shaking chills, and profuse diaphoresis (sweating).
- Sharp, pleuritic chest pain that worsens with deep breathing or coughing.
- Shortness of breath (dyspnea), rapid shallow breathing (tachypnea), or difficulty breathing.
- Confusion, disorientation, or altered mental status (especially in elderly patients).
- Muscle aches, headache, fatigue, loss of appetite, and nausea.

## Diagnostic Criteria & Triage
Diagnosis is confirmed by chest X-ray showing focal pulmonary infiltrate or consolidation, combined with clinical findings (fever, crackles on pulmonary auscultation, leukocytosis >11,000 /mcL). Patients with respiratory rate >30 breaths/min, blood pressure <90/60 mmHg, or oxygen saturation <92% SpO2 require urgent inpatient hospitalization and antibiotic therapy.
