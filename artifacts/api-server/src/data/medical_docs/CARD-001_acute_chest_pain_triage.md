# Acute Chest Pain & Coronary Triage Guidelines
Document ID: CARD-001
Source: ACC/AHA Clinical Practice Guidelines for Evaluation of Chest Pain
Publisher: American College of Cardiology & American Heart Association
Document Type: clinical_guideline
Version: 2.0

## Triage Protocol & Heart Attack Warning Signs
Acute chest pain or discomfort is a cardinal warning sign of a heart attack (myocardial infarction) or acute coronary syndrome (ACS). Key warning signs include:
- Crushing pressure, squeezing, fullness, or pain in the center of the chest lasting more than a few minutes.
- Pain radiating to the left arm, shoulders, neck, jaw, back, or stomach.
- Sudden shortness of breath (dyspnea) with or without chest discomfort.
- Profuse cold sweat (diaphoresis), nausea, vomiting, lightheadedness, or sudden dizziness.
- Severe fatigue, anxiety, or a feeling of impending doom.

## Emergency Action Criteria
Immediate emergency room triage (calling 911 / emergency services) is mandatory for any individual exhibiting acute chest pain or myocardial infarction warning signs. Do not wait for symptoms to resolve.

## Clinical Interpretation & Diagnostic Rules
A normal resting electrocardiogram (ECG) does not rule out acute myocardial ischemia. Cardiac troponin biomarker elevation combined with ischemic clinical history represents a high-risk diagnosis requiring urgent catheterization evaluation.
